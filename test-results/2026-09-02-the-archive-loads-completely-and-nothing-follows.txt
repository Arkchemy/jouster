WHAT THIS REFRAMES
------------------
Three readings of [e+0x14] > [e+0x18] have now been tried and two are dead:

    +0x18 is the header's file count      NO -- 0x6a appears nowhere
    +0x18 is set by the TOC parse         NO -- the TOC parse ran, once,
                                          successfully, on this archive

The third fits everything seen so far and is deliberately not being treated
as established: that +0x14 and +0x18 are "work issued" against "work
wanted", and a limit of zero means nothing is asking for more. On that
reading the archive did not fail -- it finished, and whatever should have
requested the next file never did. The guard is not a symptom of a broken
archive, it is the archive correctly reporting that its queue is empty.

If that is right, the fault is upstream of everything measured so far, in
whoever consumes an archive once its table of contents is ready.

addWork is the function that queues work onto the list startNewTasks walks.
If it reads 1, exactly one piece of work was ever queued -- the read that
already completed -- and nothing has asked for anything since. That is the
measurement that separates "the archive is broken" from "nobody wants
anything".
