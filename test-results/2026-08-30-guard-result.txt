2026-08-30 — the guard, and what was actually one bug
=====================================================

Suppressing 6,625 stray writes removed essentially every symptom measured
this week. Before, and after:

                              before        after
    calls executed            710,046   1,337,074    +88%
    cur_mem_ctx                0x80001   0x4400170    survives
    memwatch transitions             5           2    never cleared
    defpool nullret                  1           0    all 18 resolve
    poolarg nullarg                  1           0
    badbuf hits                 24,602           0
    dispatchmiss                73,308          53    -99.9%
    nullwrite                    1,726          66
    reallocCommon calls          2,420      42,260    pool 0x4500274, real

So the null context, the null pool, the 24,000 bad buffers and the 73,000
silent dispatches were not four problems. They were one failed allocation,
seen four ways, and every one of them dissolved when the loop that scribbled
through the data section was contained. Days of investigation went into
symptoms; the guard is what proved they were symptoms.

The table also settles which fault it is. flag (the byte at pool+8) is 1 on
all 18 calls including the failing one, so reallocCommon's early bail --
`lbz r9, 8(r3)` / `beq`, which returns null before attempting anything -- is
ruled out. The pool is switched on and the arena is what refused.

WHAT REMAINS, WHICH IS REAL
---------------------------
Two things, and neither is the old cascade.

1. nullbucket hits=2. Allocations still fail. Six cycles of three allocations
   from pool 0x4500274, buffers climbing ~971KB a cycle; five cycles fit and
   the sixth does not. That is a sizing or lifetime question about the pool,
   not a translation bug.

2. The boot now runs to 1.34M calls and settles at a dead-steady ~20,025
   calls per 60 frames. Constant to a fraction of a percent is a loop, not
   slow progress. Registration is still 124 classes, so it is looping rather
   than advancing.
