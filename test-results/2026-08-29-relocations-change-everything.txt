That last one matters most. The metaobject global that took most of a day to
chase, and that "nothing in 8.7M lines writes", now reads a real pointer. It
was always written -- by a relocation the loader never read.

The workarounds built on top of those symptoms are gone with them, and
deliberately were not forced back in after regeneration: the typeIndex-2
fallback fires 0 times and the module-range low-bound patch no longer matches
its site. Both were compensating for pointers that should have come from data
relocations.

A DIFFERENT DEATH, AND A BETTER ONE
-----------------------------------
The run ends at frame 2760 rather than running the full 7200, and for the
first time it is not a hang:

    mem: fail=1
    MEMAllocFromExpHeapEx (out of space) requested=180355080
        heap_base=0x4000000 heap_size=121634816 heap_used=12585476

The game asked for 180,355,080 bytes -- 172MB -- from a 116MB heap. That is
the first genuine allocation failure this project has produced, and it is a
sign of progress: the engine is now getting far enough to make real
allocations of a real size. On a Wii U, MEM2 is 2GB and a 172MB engine pool is
unremarkable.

Registration reads 96 rather than 120, which is not a regression to be
explained away -- the two numbers are not measuring the same thing. The run
ended at frame 2760 instead of 7200, so it had less than half the time, and
the type indices now come back varied and plausible (8, 3, 10, 36 for igQueue,
igRawRefList, SaveFlushInfoMetaField, igCafeStorageDevice) where they used to
be 2 and 3 almost exclusively. igCafeStorageDevice registering at all is new.
