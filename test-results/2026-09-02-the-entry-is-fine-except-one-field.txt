THE HYPOTHESIS IS DEAD
----------------------
The previous entry proposed that [e+0x18] should hold the 106 files the
archive header reports, and that a zero there meant the header was read but
never parsed into this object.

    occurrences of 0x6a anywhere in the entry or in what +8 points to: 0

106 is not in this object. It is not at +0x18, and it is not hiding at some
other offset either. Whatever +0x18 is, it is not the header's file count,
and the neat explanation goes in the bin.

Worth saying plainly because the reasoning felt strong: a valid header with
106 files had just been proven to land correctly in guest memory, a limit of
zero was sitting one guard away, and the two numbers wanted to be the same
number. They are not related at all.

WHAT THE DUMP DOES SHOW
-----------------------
The entry is NOT a barely-initialised husk. It has a vtable, a flags word,
three live pointers, a back-pointer at +0x2c, and plausible counts at +0x28
(23) and +0x3c (35). Something built this properly.

And in the whole populated region up to +0x28, exactly one field is zero
besides +0x24: the one the guard compares against. Everything the
constructor touched, it filled. The field that gates the work is the field
that did not get set.

It arrives that way. At frame 0 the object does not exist; by frame 9,060 it
is complete with f14=1 f18=0, and not one of those sixteen words changes for
the remaining 16,000 frames. Nothing degrades it -- it is born like this.

One observation, offered as an observation only: the object at +8 has 0x800
at its own +0x18. Its vtable word differs from the entry's (0x11cc40 against
0x11e7e8), so they are probably different types and the offsets probably do
not mean the same thing. Recorded because it is cheap to record and because
guessing that they DO match is exactly the kind of inference that just cost
a round.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY LEFT
---------------------
Two questions, and only one of them can be answered from our side:

    what SHOULD [e+0x18] be on a working boot?      -> not answerable here
    who writes it?                                   -> not answerable here,
                                                        because on our side
                                                        nothing writes it

Both are exactly what the Cemu oracle exists for, which is now running
against the full 6.1 GB content with a breakpoint on igArchive::startNewTasks.
Reading those two fields on a boot that works settles the first, and a
watchpoint on +0x18 settles the second.

METHOD NOTE
-----------
The dump was added specifically because "+0x18 is the file count" was a guess
about a field offset, and this project has repeatedly paid for treating those
as known. It cost one build and killed the guess outright. Cheaper than
building a fix on top of it.
