WHY THE INTERPROCEDURAL CASE IS ALSO CLOSED
-------------------------------------------
Worth stating, because it was very nearly missed. A store through a pointer
passed as a function argument is invisible to a scanner that resets its
register tracking at function boundaries -- and 0x10134560 is address-taken 17
times with 0x101345E8 sitting exactly 0x88 past it, which is the classic shape
of "pass a struct pointer to an init function".

What closes it is that conquertron folds relocations into fully-resolved
synthetic constants. `addi r3, r3, 0x4560` emits `ctx->r[3] = ctx->r[3];` -- a
no-op, because the displacement is already in the preceding lis. So in
synthetic space, ANY reference to this global, whether to load it, store it,
or take its address to hand to a callee, must materialise the constant 435928
somewhere. It appears exactly once in 8.7 million lines, and that once is the
read.

REMAINING GAP, STATED RATHER THAN GLOSSED
-----------------------------------------
One path is not excluded: a callee receiving a *different* nearby base
constant and storing at a large displacement. Synthetic .bss is linear
(&.bss+321000 - &.bss+306328 = 435928 - 421256 = 14672 exactly), so
`stw rX, 0x3950(r3)` with r3 = 421256 would land on the target and no scan
here would see it. 0x3950 is a legal 16-bit displacement, though a very large
one for a struct field. Ruling it out needs real interprocedural analysis.

WHAT THIS MEANS
---------------
The recompilation is faithful: full text coverage, consistent folding. The
global really is read once and written never. That makes "an uninitialised
pointer" less likely to be the whole story than it looked, and moves suspicion
toward the read at 215db84 executing on a path that should not be reached yet
-- i.e. the NULL is a symptom of an earlier wrong branch, not the origin.

That is a different investigation from the one that has been running, and it
should start from what selects that path, not from the global.
