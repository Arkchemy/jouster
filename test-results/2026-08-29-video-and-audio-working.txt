    [ff] video: binkvideo 1280x720

    [ff] audio streaming: 843816 frames (17.6 s) at 48000 Hz

526 frames at 29.97fps is 17.55 seconds, against 17.6 seconds of audio. Every
packet decoded, nothing dropped, picture and sound in step. Confirmed on
hardware by the owner: "the bash video played flawlessly".

THE THREE FAULTS THAT GOT IT THERE, ALL MINE
---------------------------------------------
1. Sound stopped after a few seconds. Two separate causes: the clip is 32000
   Hz and audout only runs at 48000, so the samples played at the wrong rate;
   and the whole clip went in as ONE buffer when audout takes a queue. Fixed
   with swresample (562,560 -> 843,816 frames, exactly 1.5x) and 0.256s
   slices with four in flight, topped up from the video loop.
2. Video ran at roughly 130fps. Not truncation -- all 526 frames were being
   presented, just with nothing pacing them, because swscale made the
   conversion fast enough to outrun real time. Now paced from the stream's own
   avg_frame_rate.
3. Frames were slow before that because the YUV to RGBA conversion was a
   hand-written per-pixel loop over 921,600 pixels. swscale was already linked
   and unused.

BOOT MOVIE: THE DIAGNOSTIC PAID FOR ITSELF
-------------------------------------------
    [ff] presented 0 frames (0 packets sent, 0 receive errors)

Zero PACKETS, not zero frames -- so the demuxer never yielded anything and the
decoder was never at fault. The file is a valid Annex-B stream: SPS, PPS, SEI,
one IDR and 61 non-IDR slices, 62 frames.

The cause is av_seek_frame. Raw Annex-B has no index and no timestamps, and
seeking one leaves the demuxer unable to read on. bash.mov survives the same
call because Bink is a real container. With no audio stream the decode pass
returns without reading anything, so there was nothing to rewind past: the
seek was pure damage. Now only performed when the audio pass actually consumed
packets, with a reopen if it fails.

Worth noting: "presented 0 frames" alone could not tell those apart. Splitting
the counter into packets-sent and receive-errors turned one ambiguous number
into an unambiguous one, and it cost a single line.
